# Motion-aware face compositing

Use this procedure for an authorized facial replacement in single-view footage when gaze shifts, blinks, or abrupt movement make a tracked edge visibly slip. Work in decoded source-pixel coordinates and keep the destination frame as the reference for timing and for areas the swap should preserve.

1. Align the selected destination face to a canonical crop with sparse landmarks. Confirm the selection on an actual decoded frame at the playhead. Lock association to that face and return the original frame when association becomes unreliable.
2. Parse the current aligned crop to obtain a bounded semantic blend mask. To inspect the facial plane, partition the visible mask into ordered relative-depth bands from the face mesh. Draw the bands as individually toggleable overlays registered to the selected frame. Treat mesh depth as an estimate from the camera view. Measured anatomy and occluded surface reconstruction require different inputs.
3. Protect the eyes with the union of the current semantic eye labels and eyelid contours from the current mesh. The contours protect closed eyes when the parser finds little visible iris or sclera. Feather outward from the protected area and force its alpha to zero again after projection into the destination frame. Inspect the original and the preview at the same playhead.
4. Preserve the performer's own mouth interior from the current target frame. The swap generator renders teeth soft and loses the tongue, so keep teeth, the dark mouth cavity, and a visible tongue from the destination. Use the face mesh's inner lip outline confirmed by the face parser to gate the opening: closed lips stay fully swapped. Detect a stuck-out tongue by colour (redder than nearby skin, touching and reaching past the lips) and keep it along with the strip of lip it covers. Flow tracking uses only points that move with the skull, so talking and smiling do not pull the head estimate. Score preservation with [make_expression_face_clip.py](../../full-stack-app-creation/assets/footage-editor/tests/make_expression_face_clip.py) and [mouth_expression_benchmark.py](../../full-stack-app-creation/assets/footage-editor/tests/mouth_expression_benchmark.py).
5. Handle occlusion by objects in front of the face. The face parser has no hand class, so it labels fingers at the mouth as skin or lips. An XSeg occlusion model marks what covers the face in every frame, and the swap mask leaves those pixels untouched. The same mask keeps the motion-blur match measuring the face rather than a fast-moving hand. While a hand hides the mouth corners, carry the alignment rigidly with the eyes and nose instead of using the face mesh's guess, and ignore flow points on the hand so the crop does not jump. Score with [make_feeding_face_clip.py](../../full-stack-app-creation/assets/footage-editor/tests/make_feeding_face_clip.py), which draws a hand bringing food to the mouth with exact occluder masks.
6. Keep a short history of aligned edge samples. Weight them with a Gaussian kernel over age, relative expressive-landmark depth, and luminance residual, then reduce their influence as motion increases. Confine the result to the current frame's semantic support. Use narrower landmark influence around the eyes and clear temporal history at cuts, fast movement, and tracking gaps. Apply color matching chiefly along the seam.
7. Test the response to an abrupt position change, stale eye positions, a change in mask coverage, and a tracking gap. Compare registered previews during scrubbing and playback. Verify every decoded output frame and its timing, plus exact pixel equality wherever the applied mask has zero alpha.

The reference implementation uses projected Gaussian weighting inspired by [4D Gaussian Splatting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08528). Its state consists of image-plane observations, relative depth, and time. A trained, view-synthesizing 4D scene would require a separate reconstruction and validation workflow. The eye contours and depth estimates come from [MediaPipe Face Mesh](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/wiki/MediaPipe-Face-Mesh).

## Tracking faces through action

Per-frame detection is the usual cause of a swap that blurs, wobbles or lands on the wrong face during fast movement. The detector's five points jitter from frame to frame, drop out under motion blur, and treat a whip pan as a new face. Track the face across the clip in two passes before rendering anything.

1. **Analyse first, render second.** In the first pass, predict each frame's face from the previous one with pyramidal Lucas-Kanade flow over points inside the face. Keep points that pass a forward-backward check and fit a similarity transform with RANSAC. Choose the detection nearest the prediction rather than the most confident one. Recheck identity against the selected face's embedding at intervals so the track does not slide onto a neighbour.
2. **Place the swap from the dense mesh.** Derive the five alignment points from a face mesh run on an upright crop around the predicted face: iris centres, nose tip and mouth corners. Averaging eye contours or using a nose-bridge point biases the alignment and lowers identity similarity.
3. **Carry short detector gaps with flow.** Allow about half a second of flow-only frames. Detect shot changes with a colour-histogram correlation, so a fast pan that keeps the same scene is not treated as a cut.
4. **Fill gaps only when the face is slow.** Interpolate across a short gap only if both ends move slowly, for example under about 0.12 of the eye distance per frame. A linear guess through a shake or whip lands far from the face. An unswapped frame looks better than a misplaced one.
5. **Smooth without lag.** Run a One Euro filter forwards and backwards. Drive its cutoff from a centred speed estimate, such as a robust maximum over a few neighbouring frames, so a still face is smoothed heavily while a fast one is barely filtered. A causal filter trails behind fast motion. Reset the filter at cuts and gaps.
6. **Match the recording's look.** Blur the generated face along the measured motion direction. Choose the length by comparing directional gradient sharpness of the target face against a few candidate shutter fractions. When the swap is upscaled into a large face, add back the target's high-frequency skin texture on skin labels only, so the new eyes, lips and nose are kept.
7. **Score tracking against ground truth.** Build a synthetic clip from a portrait with a known transform per frame. Include a pause, a whip pan, a shake with roll, a zoom, a squash that imitates a head turn, and a blurred whip. Integrate sub-frame renders across the shutter so the blur is real. Report mean and 95th-percentile placement error in eye-distance units, frame-to-frame jitter (second difference), and coverage with detections deliberately removed. Compare the new tracker with the old one on the same clip.
